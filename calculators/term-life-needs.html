How it's calculated

Liquid net worth grows each year at the rate of return, plus that year's savings (savings rate × gross income, added at year end). Income grows at the income growth rate. All figures are nominal.

Income multiple: need = multiple × that year's income + lump-sum needs − liquid net worth. Human life value: need = present value of the income replacement share of every remaining paycheck until retirement, discounted at the HLV discount rate, + lump-sum needs − liquid net worth. Need never goes below $0.

Declining term starts at the peak need (rounded up). With auto-fit on, it searches every possible level period and picks the one, with the fastest decline that still covers each year, that buys the fewest coverage-dollar-years. The premium is modeled as level, based on the starting face, which is how most declining term is sold. Ladder tests every combination of up to the chosen number of 10/15/20/25/30-year policies and keeps the cheapest that covers every year. Ladder and level policies are assumed to be cancelled once the client is self-insured.

The premium rates are placeholders. Swap in real quotes for the client's age, health class and face band; the break-even rate in the comparison tells you what a declining term quote has to beat.
