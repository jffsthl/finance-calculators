The rule estimate is N ÷ rate (in percent). The exact answer for annual compounding is ln 2 ÷ ln(1 + r).

Years to double at each annual return. Error = rule estimate − exact (positive means the rule overstates the time). The closest rule in each row is in green.

Rule error by rate

Estimate − exact, in years. The Rule of 72 is closest from about 5% to 25% and almost exact near 8%. The Rule of 70 is closer below about 5%. The Rule of 69.3 runs about 0.35 years short at every rate, because it is exact only for continuous compounding.

How it works

  • Years to double: exact t = ln 2 ÷ ln(1 + r) with r as a decimal. Rule of N: t ≈ N ÷ (r × 100).
  • Required rate: exact r = 21/t − 1. Rule of N: r ≈ N ÷ t percent.
  • 69.3 ≈ 100 × ln 2, so it is exact for continuous compounding and slightly low for yearly compounding. 72 is used because it has many divisors and happens to be most accurate around 6–10%.
  • Returns are before taxes, fees and inflation. To double purchasing power, use the real (after-inflation) return.
