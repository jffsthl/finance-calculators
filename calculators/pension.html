Pension Retiree Inflation-Tilt Backtest

A married couple living mostly on a fixed nominal pension plus CPI-indexed Social Security. Does tilting their IRA toward TIPS and natural resources protect them from inflation? Every 30-year retirement starting in January 1927–1996, replayed with 2026 dollars and 2026 tax law.

One inflation episode. The high-inflation tercile is essentially the 1966–82 Great Inflation (start years about 1953–1975, overlapping windows), and TIPS before 1997 are synthetic. Treat the inflation results as one stress test, not a statistical estimate.

Method

  • Windows. Rolling 30-year retirements starting each January, 1927–1996 (70 windows; the 1996 window ends Dec 2025). Annual steps: all cash flows at the start of each year, then that calendar year's portfolio return (monthly compounding inside the year, expense ratios deducted monthly, rebalanced to target each year).
  • Household. Pension fixed in nominal dollars (no COLA). Social Security grows with the window's CPI. Year-1 spending = (1 − savings rate) × year-1 after-tax income, then grows with CPI. With the defaults that is $50,000 + $40,000 − $4,616 tax = $85,384 after tax, and spending of $81,115.
  • Surplus and shortfall. A surplus is added to the portfolio (treated as a taxable contribution in the same mix). A shortfall is taken as a grossed-up traditional-IRA withdrawal, solved by bisection so pension + SS + W − tax = spending. "Depleted" = the balance can't cover that year's gross withdrawal; ending wealth is then 0. RMDs are ignored.
  • Taxes. 2026 married-filing-jointly brackets (10–37% at $24,800 / $100,800 / $211,400 / $403,550 / $512,450 / $768,700, IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32) and the $32,200 standard deduction plus 2 × $1,650 for 65+, all scaled by the window's CPI. The OBBBA $6,000 senior deduction is excluded. No state tax (Texas). Social Security taxation uses the IRC §86 $32,000 / $44,000 thresholds held fixed in nominal dollars, as in law, so more SS becomes taxable over time.
  • Expense ratios. Stock 0.03%, Agg 0.03%, TIPS 0.05%, nat res 0.40%.
  • Statistics. Success = never short of a gross withdrawal. Ending and path-minimum wealth are real (2026 dollars), pre-tax. Percentiles use linear interpolation. Inflation terciles split the windows in range by 30-year cumulative CPI.

Data, splices and proxies

  • CPI: BLS CPI-U (FRED CPIAUCNS). October 2025 was never published (government shutdown), so it is interpolated between September and November.
  • US stock: CRSP value-weighted total market (Ken French Mkt-RF + RF) from July 1926; October 2026 spliced from VTSMX.
  • Agg: synthetic until 1986: 70% Treasury par bond at the 10-year yield + 30% corporate par bond at the average of Moody's AAA and BAA less 0.20%/yr default loss, both 7-year maturity. VBMFX from January 1987. Against VBMFX the proxy has annual correlation 0.94 but ran about 0.3–0.4%/yr above it.
  • TIPS: synthetic until June 2000 (a 10-year real par bond repriced at the assumed real yield, plus CPI), VIPSX from July 2000. R1 uses 10-year nominal − trailing 10-year CPI through 1981, then the Cleveland Fed 10-year real rate. R2–R4 use a constant real yield; R5 clips R1 to −1%…5%.
  • Natural resources: cap-weighted Ken French industry proxy (agriculture, mines, coal, oil, gold from 1963, steel) until September 2010, then GNR (S&P Global Natural Resources). The proxy is US-only and energy-heavy; it beat GNR by about 3.4%/yr in their 2010–26 overlap. The haircut toggle subtracts 2%/yr before October 2010.

Caveats

  • The Great Inflation is a single episode. High-inflation windows overlap heavily and all live through 1966–82, so inflation-regime results are one scenario.
  • TIPS before 1997 are synthetic. TIPS didn't exist; the result depends on the assumed real yield. Under R3 (constant 1%) the TIPS-only mix C is no better than B. R1 produces −3% real yields in 1946–55 and a +7% spike in 1981–82.
  • The nat-res proxy is optimistic versus a global natural-resources fund (US-only, energy-heavy before 2010). Use the haircut toggle to stress it.
  • The Agg proxy is about 0.3–0.4%/yr optimistic versus VBMFX, which flatters B, D and F slightly.
  • Every window uses 2026 tax law and dollar amounts; there is no historical tax law. Ending wealth is pre-tax. RMDs are ignored.

Research backtest for discussion, not a forecast or advice. Past sequences of returns and inflation won't repeat. Source study: pension-bt (November 2026).
