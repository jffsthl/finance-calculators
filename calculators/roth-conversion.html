Markdown. Rewritten on every run.

What this models, and what it skips

  • 2026 federal law: ordinary brackets, standard and age-65 deductions, the $6,000 senior deduction through 2028, capital-gain stacking, Social Security taxation, NIIT, RMDs from the Uniform Lifetime Table, and IRMAA with its two-year lookback.
  • Each spouse owns an IRA with its own RMD start age. At the first death the survivor rolls the other IRA into their own, files single, keeps the larger Social Security benefit and half of any pension, and spends 80% as much. A couple's plan runs until the younger spouse, or the survivor, reaches the plan age.
  • Everything is in today's dollars. Brackets are assumed to keep pace with inflation. Social Security and NIIT thresholds are not indexed, so they shrink in real terms each year.
  • Spending comes from taxable first, then pre-tax, then Roth. For couples of different ages the engine tests converting from each spouse's IRA first and keeps the better order. The do-nothing path draws from the older spouse's IRA first. The score is after-tax wealth in the final year: Roth and taxable at full value, pre-tax net of the heirs' tax rate.
  • The taxable account pays a 1.8% qualified dividend and is otherwise untaxed until sold. Pensions are assumed to rise with inflation. A joint return is filed for the year of a death, and the deceased spouse's RMD for that year is still taken.
  • Skipped: per-spouse Roth and taxable ownership, the Joint Life RMD table for a spouse more than 10 years younger, the Roth five-year rules, QCDs, ACA subsidies before 65, asset location, state tax beyond a flat rate, and the 10% early-withdrawal penalty.
  • Example households are invented. This is a planning sketch to check against your own software before it reaches a client.
