Fixed-Income Risk-On / Risk-Off

Shift a bond portfolio between high yield, core bonds and Treasuries depending on how wide credit spreads are versus their history. Backtested monthly since 1987, unlevered, with no look-ahead.

How it works

States. Neutral holds 40% high yield and 60% Vanguard Total Bond Market (VBMFX). Risk-on (80/20) starts when the spread is above its 70th percentile and has already come at least 15% off its 3-month peak, so you buy after the panic has turned, not into it. It ends when the spread falls below its 10-year median, but no earlier than 12 months after entry. Risk-off (0% high yield, 50% VBMFX, 50% Treasury fund) starts when the spread is below its 20th percentile and has widened at least 15% off its 6-month low. It ends when the percentile goes back above 40.

No look-ahead. Everything uses month-end values. The state decided at the end of a month sets the next month's weights, and the portfolio is rebalanced to those weights monthly. Percentiles use an expanding window: today's spread is ranked against all history up to today. Each month the current state's exit is checked first; if the portfolio is then neutral, risk-on entry is checked before risk-off. The two entry conditions can never both be true at once.

Signals. The main signal is Moody's Baa corporate yield minus the 10-year Treasury yield (FRED BAA10Y, month-end). Its percentile history starts in 1953 (monthly averages before 1986), giving a 33-year burn-in. Because spreads were structurally lower in 1953–1985, the Baa spread never ranked below its 20th percentile after 1986, so this signal never goes risk-off. The "history from 1986 only" option uses a 5-year burn-in (backtest from 1991) and does trigger risk-off. The ICE BofA high-yield option-adjusted spread is the more direct measure. FRED now only publishes its last 3 years, so its history from Dec 1996 is rebuilt from an archived FRED copy and a CC BY 4.0 mirror, with 7 missing month-ends in 2021 estimated from the Baa spread. It gets a 5-year burn-in, so that backtest starts in 2002.

Small sample. There have been only a handful of credit cycles since 1987, and each signal produces 4–7 episodes. Results lean heavily on a few post-crisis rebounds (2002, 2009, 2016, 2020). The parameters are fixed in advance, not optimized, but treat the outperformance as suggestive rather than proven. Fund returns are total returns net of fund expenses. There are no taxes or trading costs.
